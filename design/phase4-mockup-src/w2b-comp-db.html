Fancy Comp: level history on a dB scale (mockup)

docs/comp.md, "Level history in dB", Decisions 1 to 3 A. New things in dashed yellow, on the plugin's real screenshot (1x coordinates).
1 · Before: the History page today (v1.7.0, linear)
The CI screenshot, unchanged: a beat at 120 BPM into 3:1 at -12 dB. The threshold is a quarter of the way out, and everything under about -30 dBFS is a hairline at the centre.
The page, the zoom switch, Grid, peak and the GR line from the top (0 to 24 dB, right) all stay as they are.
2 · After: the same beat on a dB scale
Only the waveforms and the threshold move: each column's peak is drawn at its level in dB, from -60 dB at the centre line to 0 dB at the edges (Decision 1 A, mirrored; Decision 2 A, a fixed 60 dB). Faint level lines every 12 dB, with their values in grey at the left edge; the GR scale stays at the right in yellow. On this scale the gap between the grey and teal edges is the gain reduction itself, at 3.5 px per dB.
No new control (Decision 3 A: dB only, the linear view goes). Not a parameter and nothing saved; the recorder and its columns are unchanged, so this is a paint change in fancy::ui::LevelHistory only. The tooltip and the manual say the scale.
3 · Why: a quiet passage, both ways
A spoken phrase, syllables at -14 to -24 dBFS and a breath at -44, into 3:1 at -24 dB.
Today: linear
Proposed: dB, floor -60
The same phrase both ways. On the linear scale a syllable at -24 dBFS reaches 6 % of the half-height and the -24 dB threshold sits 8 px from the centre, so neither the reduction nor the breath at -44 can be read. In dB every syllable, the breath and the room tone at -52 are visible, and the threshold sits 60 % of the way out.
4 · Decision 1 B for comparison: one-sided, level up from the bottom
The same two signals with the level drawn up from -60 dB at the bottom edge to 0 dB at the top, as a level meter over time: twice the height per dB (7 px), one threshold line, but it is no longer a waveform, and loud peaks run into the GR line coming down from the top.
Left: the beat. Right: the spoken phrase.
